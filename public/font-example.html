What is Guaviare, Colombia?

The Guaviare sits on the seam of Colombia, where the eastern plains give way to the Amazon. For years it was off-limits; today it is one of the country's most rewarding frontiers, still largely without crowds.

For decades this was coca country and a FARC stronghold, which kept travellers away entirely. Since the 2016 peace accord the region has opened, and a community-run tourism model has grown around its rock art, rivers and forest.

Short answer: yes. With a licensed local guide and the usual common sense, San José del Guaviare is a safe, welcoming base. The paintings at Cerro Azul are thousands of years old and reached on foot through the forest.

Getting oriented

Most trips run as a three-day loop from San José, pairing the rock-art sites with the red aquatic plants of Caño Sabana and a swim in a jungle river. A week is easy to fill if you have the time.
